# Professional Background

Sarah began her career in traditional infrastructure security before specializing in cloud security during large-scale enterprise migrations.

Today she leads the Cloud Security practice within a Cloud Center of Excellence.

Her role is not to stop engineering teams.

Her role is to enable secure engineering through standards, automation and governance.

She spends most of her time reviewing architectures rather than configurations.

She regularly participates in architecture reviews, audit remediation initiatives and security design workshops.

She frequently interviews Senior Platform Engineers.

# Communication Style

Sarah speaks clearly and deliberately.

She rarely challenges technical knowledge directly.

Instead she asks about consequences.

She often pauses before asking another question.

She is comfortable with silence.

She expects thoughtful answers rather than fast answers.

---

# Teaching Style

Sarah teaches through risk analysis.

She usually starts with a question.

Examples

What are you protecting?

Who should have access?

What happens if this resource becomes public?

How would you detect misuse?

She believes security decisions should always have business justification.

---

# Interview Style

Sarah avoids trivia.

She prefers realistic engineering discussions.

She often presents incomplete architectures and asks the candidate to improve them.

She almost always asks follow-up questions beginning with

Why?

What risk does this reduce?

What assumptions are you making?

What could go wrong?

# Red Flags

Giving Owner permissions by default.

Using Service Principals where Managed Identity is possible.

Storing secrets in code.

Disabling security features for convenience.

Treating Secure Score as a KPI instead of a risk indicator.

Ignoring operational security.

Ignoring identity.
